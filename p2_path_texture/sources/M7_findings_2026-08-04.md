estimator_verification: >
  Both estimators verified against known cases BEFORE building the mechanism:
  permutation entropy — smooth trend PE~=0, white noise ~0.98, periodic ~0.43 at
  250 obs. Also verified the step-3 stability test MUST be a BLOCK bootstrap: a
  naive resample destroys the temporal ordering PE reads and falsely reports ~0
  rank-stability even on a genuine smoothness gradient (block bootstrap correctly
  reports ~0.89 on the same gradient). PE and SampEn measure disorder through
  DIFFERENT lenses (ordinal/rank vs amplitude/tolerance) — their agreement is
  evidence the finding is in the data, not the method.

result: >
  DECISION: REJECT. Entropy is not a usable path-consistency signal on NSE daily
  returns. The gated design STOPPED at step 1 and computed NO performance number.

  STEP 1 (distinctness from DGW): entropy coef UNCONTROLLED -0.00012, NW t=-0.29;
  +DGW control -0.00005, t=-0.12. The gate fired, but the honest read is stronger
  than "redundant with DGW": the UNCONTROLLED t of -0.29 means entropy had
  essentially NO forward-return predictive power to begin with. There was no
  signal for the DGW control to explain away. The "reinvented frog-in-the-pan"
  label the gate prints is therefore an understatement — entropy does not predict
  returns at all, so the frog-in-the-pan comparison is moot.
  STEP 2 (vol): not reached (step 1 already failed).
  STEP 3 (stability): not reached as a gate, but measured as a diagnostic (below).
  STEP 4 (performance): NOT computed, by design.

structural_finding: >
  The estimator-INDEPENDENT reason entropy has no signal: real NSE daily returns
  have NEAR-MAXIMAL permutation entropy across the ENTIRE universe. Every stock's
  PE sits in a 0.046-wide band (0.95-1.0; spot-check: smoothest RELIGARE 0.950,
  jumpiest BSE 0.996). At daily frequency the ordinal up/down structure of Indian
  stock returns is close to indistinguishable from white noise. There is neither
  meaningful cross-sectional variation in "smoothness" nor stable measurement of
  it. Multi-month block-bootstrap rank stability = 0.440 (12 months spanning
  2011-2024, min 0.411 / max 0.477) — far below the 0.6 tradeability floor, and
  robustly so (not a one-month fluke). The intuitive "smooth trend vs jumpy trend"
  distinction largely DOES NOT EXIST as a stable, measurable cross-sectional
  property at this frequency and series length.

  TWO INDEPENDENT FAILURES pointing the same way: (a) no forward-return power
  (t=-0.29), (b) not stably measurable (rank stability 0.44). This is a STRONGER
  rejection than "redundant with a known signal" — the premise itself does not
  hold in this market at daily frequency.

insight: >
  Path-smoothness is not a usable signal on NSE daily returns because daily
  returns carry near-maximal ordinal disorder UNIVERSALLY — all stocks cluster at
  PE 0.95-1.0. Two independent failures (no predictive power t=-0.29; unstable
  ranking 0.44) reinforce one conclusion: the smooth-vs-jumpy distinction does not
  exist as a stable, measurable cross-sectional property at daily frequency. The
  gated design (distinctness/stability BEFORE performance) is what prevented a
  meaningless performance number from being computed and mistaken for a finding —
  had we led with the double-sort, we would have sorted on noise and possibly
  reported a spurious "significant" spread.

incidental_observations: >
  (1) METHODOLOGICAL: the gate log's auto-printed label ("reinvented
  frog-in-the-pan") was less accurate than the raw numbers — the uncontrolled
  t=-0.29 shows there was no signal to relabel. Lesson: read the uncontrolled
  coefficient before accepting a redundancy-gate's own verdict; "died under
  control X" and "never had signal" look similar in a surviving-fraction number
  but mean different things. (2) PE and SampEn (ordinal vs amplitude estimators)
  AGREE on near-maximal disorder — cross-method agreement is why we can attribute
  the flatness to the data, not the estimator. This is the pre-emptive answer to
  "would a different estimator find it": two different-lens estimators already say
  no. (3) The multi-month stability (0.44, tight 0.41-0.48 band across 13 years)
  is remarkably constant — the near-maximal-entropy property of NSE daily returns
  is a stable structural feature, not regime-dependent. (4) DGW %-positive-days
  column now built and cached (hypotheses/momentum/enhancements_M7_.../cache/) —
  reusable shared column for future mechanisms.
