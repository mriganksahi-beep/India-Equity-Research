# M5 — Life-cycle / trend-aging (return leg + crash leg, SEPARATE) — FINDINGS

id: momentum_enh_M5_lifecycle_trend_aging
date: 2026-08-02
mechanism: M5 (momentum enhancement batch)
hypothesis: >
  Two payoffs, kept strictly apart. (a) RETURN leg: younger/less-mature trends
  forward-outperform mature ones. (b) CRASH leg: aged trends carry more drawdown
  / negative skew (the crowding->crash link — CONTESTED, treated as open, not
  assumed). Never merged into one metric (a crash-avoidance benefit is invisible
  in Sharpe if young trends are also higher-vol).
category: momentum / trend life-cycle / crash risk
mechanism_tags: trend_age, maturity, 52wk_high, moving_average_cross, crowding,
  crash_risk, drawdown, skew, orthogonalization

data_and_universe: >
  Nifty 500 PIT universe, v3 panel (feature_panel_with_pit_v2, 3,328,132 rows,
  2010-2026). PIT filter in_nifty500_flag == 1. 8 corp-action tickers screened
  from ALL path signals (a split makes the 252-day high meaningless across the
  boundary). Monthly rebalance, forward 21d returns. Maturity proxies (convention
  HIGHER = MORE MATURE): PRIMARY 52-week-high nearness (George-Hwang, price /
  252-day high); SECONDARY (robustness) days since 200-day-MA up-cross and days
  since golden cross (50>200), both right-censored when a name is in an uptrend
  the whole window. Return leg = quintiles on maturity RESIDUAL to the 12-1
  signal; crash leg = terciles on RAW maturity.

orthogonalization_proof: >
  The return leg sorts on maturity ORTHOGONALIZED to momentum (trend age is
  mechanically correlated with accumulated momentum; without this we'd re-measure
  momentum). Verified in validation: raw corr(momentum, proxy) was +0.538
  (high52), +0.523 (200MA), +0.369 (golden); after orthogonalization ALL THREE
  dropped to +/-0.0000. The return-leg buckets are genuinely age-independent-of-
  momentum. Primary proxy hard-gates the run on this; robustness proxies warn.

result: >
  DECISION: REJECT as a tradeable enhancement. Return leg is a dead null; crash
  leg is a real characterization but yields no tradeable rule.

  RETURN LEG (orthogonalized, quintiles Q1 young -> Q5 mature, %/mo):
    high52 (HEADLINE): 1.31 1.42 1.57 1.52 1.33; young-minus-mature -0.021%/mo,
      t=-0.05, 95% CI [-0.64, +0.65]. DEAD NULL — a faint mid-hump, no gradient.
    200-MA age: young-minus-mature -0.223%/mo, t=-0.97 (wrong direction, ns).
    golden-cross age: +0.401%/mo, t=1.68 (right direction but ns, and contradicted
      by the other two — one suggestive proxy of three is not a result).
  -> Trend maturity carries NO return information independent of momentum. The
     apparent 'young trends outperform' intuition was momentum in disguise
     (confirmed: orthogonalization collapsed the spread to t=-0.05).

  CRASH LEG (RAW maturity terciles, T1 young / T3 mature):
    high52 (HEADLINE):
      T1 young  : vol 8.72%  maxDD -66.0%  skew +0.09  freq<-10% 8.4%
      T2 mid    : vol 6.20%  maxDD -49.0%  skew -0.39  freq<-10% 3.9%
      T3 mature : vol 5.06%  maxDD -31.1%  skew -1.00  freq<-10% 1.7%
    Monotonic and UNANIMOUS across all three proxies: young trends crash ~2x
    deeper and ~5x more often than mature trends. This is the OPPOSITE of the
    crowding->crash story (which predicts AGED trends crash).

vol_normalized_probe: >
  Critical follow-up (interrogates the existing crash result; NOT a new
  sub-test). Question: is young-trend drawdown EXCESS risk or just a volatility
  artifact (young trends are higher-vol)? Drawdown-per-unit-vol (DD/vol):
    high52 : young -7.57 vs mature -6.15 (gap -1.42)
    200MA  : young -9.21 vs mature -6.33 (gap -2.88)
    golden : young -7.54 vs mature -6.22 (gap -1.33)
  Young trends STILL crash deeper AFTER dividing out volatility, on all three
  proxies. This is GENUINE EXCESS crash risk, not a vol artifact. (This CORRECTS
  an initial hunch that the drawdown gap was 'largely a vol artifact' — the vol
  normalization proved the opposite. Running the probe rather than logging the
  hunch was the right call.)

crash_leg_interpretation: >
  TWO DIFFERENT KINDS OF RISK, pointing opposite ways — both real, not
  contradictory:
   - DRAWDOWN (vol-adjusted): worse in YOUNG trends = COLLAPSE risk. An unproven
     young trend can unravel entirely and stay down (a -66% cumulative fall is a
     trend that failed outright). Anti-crowding.
   - SKEW: worse in MATURE trends (-1.00 vs +0.09) = AIR-POCKET risk. Mature
     trends mostly drift up calmly, then take occasional sharp single-month hits,
     but the established trend absorbs them without cumulative collapse. A faint
     pro-crowding echo, but a DIFFERENT animal from crowding-collapse.
  The simple 'aged = crowded = crash' hypothesis is REJECTED: the deep
  (vol-adjusted) crashes live in YOUNG trends, opposite to crowding's prediction.
  Mature trends win only on single-period tail asymmetry (skew), which is not the
  cumulative collapse the crowding theory predicts.

validation_summary: >
  coverage: high52 100% of momentum-eligible; MA-age proxies ~50% (thinner by

  orthogonalization: raw corr +0.37..+0.54 -> residual +/-0.0000 on all three
    proxies (return leg is not re-measuring momentum). PASS.

    — proxy spans the range sensibly. PASS.
  corp-action screen: all 8 tickers excluded from path signals. PASS.

holdout_status: n/a (return leg null on full sample; crash leg is descriptive

decision: REJECT (return leg null; crash leg real but not tradeable)

insight: >
  In Indian momentum, trend maturity carries NO return information once
  orthogonalized against momentum — the life-cycle 'buy young trends' idea is
  momentum in disguise. On crashes, the contested crowding->crash story is
  REJECTED but the truth is more interesting: risk splits into two kinds by
  trend age. YOUNG trends carry genuine excess COLLAPSE risk (deep vol-adjusted
  drawdowns, unanimous across three proxies) — an unproven trend that fails keeps
  falling. MATURE trends carry more single-period TAIL asymmetry (negative skew)
  but absorb it without cumulative collapse. Deep crashes live in young trends,
  the OPPOSITE of what crowding predicts.

incidental_observations: >
  (1) The vol-normalization reversal is a methodological exemplar: the raw
  drawdown gap LOOKED like it could be a vol artifact (young trends are higher-
  vol), and only DD/vol normalization revealed the excess risk is genuine. Had we
  logged the hunch we'd have understated a real finding. Worth remembering: always
  vol-normalize a drawdown comparison before calling it real OR artifact.
  (2) The golden-cross return-leg proxy (t=1.68, hypothesis direction) is the lone
  suggestive return-leg result among three — dismissed as the expected noise from
  multiple proxies, consistent with the M4 lesson. Not promoted.
  (3) 2025 MA-age coverage collapses to ~16-26 names (vs ~200 typical) — the 2025
  vol soft-patch / vendor-failure window thins the MA computations too. Any future
  MA-based signal must know the 2025 window is degraded for these proxies as well.
  (4) The two-kinds-of-risk split (young=collapse, mature=air-pocket) is a genuine
  characterization of Indian momentum worth carrying into any crash-mitigation work.
