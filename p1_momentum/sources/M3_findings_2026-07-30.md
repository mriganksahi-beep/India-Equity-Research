result: >
  DECISION: REJECT. Risk-adjustment did NOT narrow the tradeability gap — it
  WIDENED it, the opposite of the hypothesis.

  Tercile Sharpe (liquidity terciles), long-short within each:
    variant                 T1 illiq  T2 mid  T3 liq  gap(T1-T3)
    plain (baseline)          0.822    0.940   0.546     0.277
    V1 vol-scaled (vol120)    0.955    0.962   0.374     0.581   <- gap DOUBLED
    V1 vol-scaled (vol60)     0.854    0.908   0.494     0.360
    V2 residual momentum      0.526    0.298   0.505     0.021
  V1 vol-scaling DROPPED the liquid tercile (0.55 -> 0.37) and RAISED the
  illiquid tercile (0.82 -> 0.96): it concentrated the effect further into
  untradeable names. Double-sort confirms: V1 long-short Sharpe 0.97 in the most
  illiquid Amihud third vs 0.46 in the most liquid.

  Headline full-sample Sharpe (vol-matched): plain 0.859, V1(vol120) 0.875,
  V1(vol60) 0.856, V2 residual 0.619, V3 const-vol 0.899. The CONFIRMATORY lift
  the developed-market literature predicts was ESSENTIALLY ABSENT in India
  (V1 ~ baseline). V2 was clearly WORSE. V3 helped but does not address
  tradeability.

collinearity_check: >
  THE decisive test (scout hierarchy: FM regression = headline; double-sort =
  picture; weight-regression = diagnostic; two controls Amihud + turnover).
  5a Fama-MacBeth with LINEAR log-Amihud + log-turnover controls: signal
     surviving fraction 104% (uncontrolled coef 0.00490 t=3.90; controlled
     0.00512 t=4.18). By the linear test the signal reads INDEPENDENT of liquidity.
  RECONCILIATION with the tercile evidence (which shows strong liquidity
     dependence): the effect is tail-concentrated, and a LINEAR liquidity control
     cannot see a tail lump. A follow-up probe added an explicit signal x
     illiquid-tercile INTERACTION term: coefficient POSITIVE (0.00219) but
     t=1.75 — SUGGESTIVE, NOT SIGNIFICANT at the pre-registered t>2 bar.
  Therefore: the OUTCOME (vol-scaling worsens tradeability) is robust and
  model-free (direct tercile observation). The MECHANISM (payoff concentrates in
  the illiquid tail, making 5a's 'independent' a linear-control blind spot) is
  the most plausible explanation but COULD NOT be established at conventional
  significance on this sample. Logged as tentative, not confirmed.
  Weight-regression (5c): V1's long book tilts marginally MORE liquid than
  plain's (-0.084 log-Amihud) — so V1 is not mechanically loading illiquid at
  selection; the illiquid concentration is in where the signal PAYS OFF, not
  where it picks. (This nuance is why the mechanism is subtle.)

decision: REJECT (hypothesis fails; effect is opposite of predicted direction)

insight: >
  In India, risk-adjusting momentum's cross-sectional rank makes the tradeability
  problem WORSE, not better — it pushes the effect DEEPER into illiquid names
  (liquid tercile Sharpe 0.55 -> 0.37; illiquid 0.82 -> 0.96). This is opposite
  to the developed-market intuition that vol-scaling improves momentum. Most
  plausible (but not significance-established) mechanism: dividing by volatility
  systematically demotes high-vol stocks; in LIQUID names high volatility IS the
  momentum signal (real, information-bearing), so demoting it discards the good
  part; in ILLIQUID names high volatility is largely thin-trading NOISE, so
  demoting it cleans the signal. Hence risk-adjustment helps exactly where you
  cannot trade and hurts exactly where you can. The developed-market vol-scaling
  benefit did not replicate in India regardless.

incidental_observations: >
  (1) V2 residual momentum was clearly WORSE (Sharpe 0.62 vs 0.86). Stripping
  [MF,SMB,HML] removes something that HELPS Indian momentum — i.e. Indian
  momentum is less purely idiosyncratic than US momentum, more entangled with
  size/value factor exposure. A real negative finding, not a null.
  (2) V3 constant-vol overlay was the only variant that improved risk-adjusted
  return (0.90 vs 0.86), but it is a volatility-TIMING overlay, not a stock
  picker, and its gain is plausibly concentrated in softening the 2020 momentum
  crash (6 months hit the 2x leverage cap; mean scalar 0.77 = mostly deleveraged).
  This REPLICATES Barroso-Santa-Clara in India — a clean confirmatory win, but on
  RISK-TIMING, orthogonal to the tradeability question M3 asked.
  (3) The signal x illiquid interaction t=1.75 is a sample-size-limited result;
  179-186 months may be too few to resolve a subtle non-linear liquidity
  interaction. A longer sample or a pooled-panel approach might resolve it.
  (4) Weight-regression shows V1 picks marginally MORE liquid names yet pays off
  in illiquid ones — the concentration is in payoff, not selection, which is why
  a naive "it just picks illiquid stocks" story is wrong.

flag_for_idea_generator: >
  The liquid-vs-illiquid ASYMMETRY of what volatility means (real signal in
  liquid names, thin-trading noise in illiquid ones) could seed a mechanism:
  a LIQUIDITY-CONDITIONAL vol adjustment — do NOT divide by vol in liquid names
  (keep raw momentum), DO divide by vol in illiquid names (denoise). This is the
  inverse of what M3 tested uniformly, and the tercile evidence predicts it could
  IMPROVE the liquid tercile rather than harm it. Green-field; route to
  Idea-Generator. SEPARATELY: V3's clean replication suggests a volatility-
  targeting overlay is worth a dedicated crash-mitigation mechanism (relates to
