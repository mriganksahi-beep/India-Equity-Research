result: >
  DECISION: REJECT the hump. It is a coarse-tercile BUCKETING ARTIFACT, not a
  genuine interior maximum. 0 of 4 primary curves (Amihud/turnover x deciles/20q)
  showed an interior maximum — and they failed decisively, not marginally: NO
  curve had BOTH a significantly-positive low-end slope and a significantly-
  negative high-end slope (the hump requirement). All boundary-slope t-stats were
  small and mostly the wrong sign.

  What the fine-bucket curves actually show (the real, monotonic-ish structure the
  3-bucket sort hid):
    - AMIHUD: momentum strength RISES toward the illiquid end (0.0017 most-liquid
      -> 0.0091 most-illiquid). A rising line, not a hump.
    - TURNOVER: momentum strength FALLS toward the high-turnover end (0.0113
      lowest-turnover -> 0.0011 highest-turnover). A falling line, not a hump.
  Both point the SAME economic direction — momentum is strongest in less-traded,
  harder-to-access names — with no interior peak. The Phase B "mid-tercile hump"
  was "low, high, low" across three coarse buckets creating an illusion of a peak
  where the fine-grained reality is a noisy monotonic gradient.

insight: >
  The Phase B mid-tercile momentum peak is NOT a real interior maximum — it is an
  artifact of collapsing a monotonic liquidity gradient into three coarse buckets.
  At decile/20-quantile resolution the relationship is monotonic-ish in BOTH
  liquidity measures: momentum is stronger in less-liquid (high-Amihud) and
  lower-turnover names, weaker in the most liquid/most-traded — with no peak in
  the middle. This is CONSISTENT with the M3 tradeability finding (momentum
  concentrates where you cannot trade). CONSEQUENCE FOR SCOPE: there is no
  mid-cap peak to explain, so the green-field information-diffusion / inverted-U
  hypothesis is NOT motivated by this data and should not be pursued on the
  strength of the "hump." The diagnostic did its job — it prevented a wild-goose
  chase after a pattern that does not exist.

  PRECISION CAVEAT: "no hump" means the curve does not rise-then-fall within the
  data range. It does NOT mean momentum is uniform across liquidity — it plainly
  is not; both measures show a strong monotonic liquidity gradient. M6 kills the
  HUMP SHAPE specifically (the thing the diffusion story needed), not the
  liquidity dependence itself.

incidental_observations: >
  (1) Amihud and turnover give OPPOSITE-signed monotonic slopes (Amihud rises to
  illiquid, turnover falls to high-turnover) yet the SAME economic conclusion
  (momentum strongest in less-traded names). That the two liquidity measures agree
  economically while sloping oppositely by construction is a useful reassurance
  the gradient is real, not a single-measure artifact. (2) A NaN-liquidity bug was
  caught on this real-data run that the first integration test missed (synthetic
  liquidity had no NaN); fixed at source (collect_month rejects non-finite

  synthetic data. (3) The lone Amihud decile-6 Sharpe of 0.96 is a good example of
  how a single noisy bucket can masquerade as a peak in a coarse view — exactly
  what Lind-Mehlum is built to see through.
